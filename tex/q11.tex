  \begin{abstract}
    An emergent Lorentzian co-metric on the product $\mathbb{R}_\tau\times\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ of an ordering line
    and a Heisenberg spatial factor carries a temporal coefficient $A_\tau$ and spatial coefficients, and a
    four-dimensional spacetime needs their relative normalisation.
    This paper determines what invariance under a spatial $\SU(2)$ action can contribute to that normalisation.
    On a cotangent fibre $\mathbb{R}_\tau\oplus W$ on which $\SU(2)$ acts trivially on the ordering line and by the
    real spin-one representation on the spatial sector, every invariant symmetric bilinear form is
    $\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,Q_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with $\alpha$ and $\beta$ independent.
    Invariance excludes mixed temporal--spatial terms but relates the two scalars by no equation.
    The ratio $A_\tau/A_H$ is therefore unconstrained, and a spatial normalisation $A_H = 2\lambda$ yields no value
    of $A_\tau$, in particular not $A_\tau = 2$.
    The conclusion does not depend on the chosen action: for every linear $\SU(2)$ action on a four-dimensional real
    space, the invariant forms are either the multiples of one definite form or a family of dimension at least two,
    so no
    $\SU(2)$-invariance condition fixes a Lorentzian co-metric up to an overall factor.
    With the ordering parameter identified with breadth-first depth through an explicit free step $\Delta\tau$,
    temporal rescalings commute with every action that is trivial on the ordering line and move the ratio, so
    equivariance cannot fix it either.
    The value $A_\tau = 2$ obtained from the Casimir eigenvalue is therefore not derived on this route: the result
    closes a derivation route, not the possibility $A_\tau = 2$.
    The missing element is named: one datum coupling the temporal and spatial normalisations, such as the step
    $\Delta\tau$ expressed in spatial units or a saturation rule for the capacity bound.
    Interpretive outlook (a reading, not a result): spatial isotropy and the unit of time are separate pieces of
    information, and the rate at which projective ordering advances relative to spatial relational distance, which
    a kinematic reading would call the invariant speed, is an independent datum that the representation theory of
    space alone does not supply.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:intro}

    \subsection{The normalisation problem}

      The effective operator of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} on $\mathbb{R}_\tau\times\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ has, under the
      spatial limit hypothesis [H-L] and the lifting hypothesis [H-lift], the rank-three co-metric
      \begin{equation}
        g^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau,\, A_H,\, A_H,\, 0)
        \label{eq:metric-intro}
      \end{equation}
      in the left-invariant frame $(\partial_\tau, \tilde X, \tilde Y, \tilde Z)$ (Q5b Theorem~5.2).
      A non-degenerate co-metric needs a new operator for the central direction (open problem Q5b-O2), and the
      values of $A_\tau$ and $A_H$ are open (open problem Q5b-O3).
      For a supplied full-rank extension whose spatial form is invariant under the full $\su(2)$ action of the
      spin-one module, Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} proves that the three spatial coefficients coincide, with common value
      $2\lambda$ and $\lambda > 0$ undetermined (Q8 Proposition~4.2); it obtains the four-dimensional form
      $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda)$ by assuming that no mixed temporal--spatial term is
      present (Q8 Corollary~6.1).

      This paper asks whether invariance under the spatial $\SU(2)$ action can also relate $A_\tau$ to the spatial
      scale.
      A Casimir-normalisation argument would set $A_\tau$ equal to the Casimir eigenvalue $2$ of the spin-one module.
      The answer is negative, and the obstruction is structural: the ordering line is a trivial isotypic component,
      and Schur's lemma leaves one free scalar per isotypic component.
      The positive content is an exact description of what invariance does fix, including the absence of mixed
      terms that Q8 assumes, and an exact statement of the one datum that is missing.

    \subsection{Main result}

      The setting is fixed in Section~\ref{sec:setup}: $W$ is the real three-dimensional spatial sector with the
      supplied real spin-one action, $\Qsp$ is its invariant positive form, and hypothesis [ACT$_\tau$] extends the
      action to $V = \Rtau\oplus W$ trivially on the ordering line.

      \begin{theorem}[Spatial $\SU(2)$ invariance leaves the temporal coefficient free]
        \label{thm:main}
        Assume \textup{[ACT$_\tau$]}.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item[(a)] Every $\SU(2)$-invariant symmetric bilinear form on $V$ is
          \[
            Q = \alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,\Qsp,
            \qquad (\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^2,
          \]
          and every such form is invariant.
          In particular no invariant form has a mixed term between $k_\tau$ and $W$.
          \item[(b)] The scalars $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are independent.
          The form has signature $(-,+,+,+)$ with $\tau$ time-like exactly when $\alpha < 0 < \beta$, and on this
          cone the ratio
          $-\alpha/\beta$ takes every positive value.
          \item[(c)] If the principal symbol of a full-rank extension of~\eqref{eq:metric-intro} is invariant, its
          co-metric in an adapted frame is $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_H)$ with $A_\tau = -\alpha$ and
          $A_H = \beta$; here $A_H$ denotes the common spatial entry, which is the horizontal coefficient
          of~\eqref{eq:metric-intro} when the adapted frame is identified with the left-invariant frame
          (the premises [T] and [ADAPT] of Q7 and Q8).
          Fixing $A_H$, for instance $A_H = 2\lambda$, imposes no condition on $A_\tau$; in particular invariance
          does not yield $A_\tau = 2$.
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{theorem}

      The conclusion is not an artefact of [ACT$_\tau$]: Proposition~\ref{prop:any-action} shows that for every linear
      $\SU(2)$ action on a four-dimensional real space the invariant forms are either the multiples of one definite
      form or a family of dimension at least two.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does not show that $A_\tau = 2$ is false.
      It shows that spatial $\SU(2)$ invariance does not derive it, and that a datum coupling the temporal and spatial
      normalisations is required.

    \subsection{Why the route stops}

      Schur's lemma determines an invariant form on an irreducible module up to one scalar.
      On a direct sum of non-isomorphic irreducibles it forbids cross terms and leaves one scalar per summand.
      The ordering parameter $\tau$ is a scalar parameter of the product on which no spatial rotation acts, so its
      covector line carries the trivial representation (Corollary~\ref{cor:no-new-rep}).
      Every multiplication by a scalar commutes with $\SU(2)$ on that line, so the temporal scalar is free.
      Relating it to the spatial scalar would require an equation between two isotypic components, and no invariance
      condition provides one.

      The same freedom appears in the ordering parameter itself.
      Identifying $\tau$ with breadth-first depth requires a temporal step $\Delta\tau$ per cascade step
      (Modelling identification~\ref{lem:internalization}).
      Rescaling $\Delta\tau$ commutes with every spatial action and multiplies the temporal coefficient
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:equivariance}), so no equivariance property of the ordering increment can fix the
      step.

    \subsection{Relation to other approaches}

      In special relativity the speed of light converts units of time into units of length, and in Lorentzian
      geometry the opening of the light cone is exactly the relative normalisation studied here.
      Programmes in which Lorentz invariance is emergent, such as the analogue-gravity
      programme~\cite{BarceloLiberatiVisser2011} and the condensed-matter scenarios of~\cite{Volovik2003}, obtain the
      limiting speed from the microscopic dynamics, not from a spatial symmetry: a rotation-invariant medium has
      rotation-invariant excitations with any propagation speed.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is the representation-theoretic form of that observation for the present carrier.
      The classification of real representations used in Proposition~\ref{prop:any-action} is
      standard~\cite{BrockerTomDieck1985,FultonHarris1991}.

    \subsection{Organisation}

      Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes notation and states the upstream inputs with their current status.
      Section~\ref{sec:internalization} states the ordering identification as a modelling input with an explicit step.
      Section~\ref{sec:equivariance} shows that temporal rescalings commute with the spatial action.
      Section~\ref{sec:schur} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and extends it to arbitrary actions.
      Section~\ref{sec:consequences} draws the consequences for the co-metric and for Q5b-O3.
      Section~\ref{sec:summary} summarises the result, gives its interpretive reading and lists open problems.

    \subsection{Objects and standing hypotheses}

      Fix a prime $q\geq 5$.
      The discrete Heisenberg group is $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ with generators $X_q,Y_q,Z_q$, and
      $n$ denotes breadth-first (BFS) depth on its Cayley graph.
      The fingerprint observable of Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} at depth $n$ for a block $b$ is
      \[
        \sigma_b(n) := \frac{\delta r_n(b)}{|S_n|},
      \]
      where $\delta r_n(b)$ is the increase, from shell $n-1$ to shell $n$, of the dimension of the Gram--Schmidt span
      of the Weil fingerprint vectors of block $b$, and $|S_n|$ is the shell size.
      It is a rank increment, not a spectral quantity; for fixed $b$ it is a real sequence indexed by $n$.

      The spatial sector $W$ is a real three-dimensional space carrying the real spin-one representation of $\SU(2)$,
      that is, the standard action of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ through the covering
      $\SU(2)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)$.
      That the real spatial sector carries this adjoint action is the hypothesis [ACT] of Q7~\cite{Beau2026q7}
      (Q7 Lemma~4.3 and Remark~4.6), also used by Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}; no source supplies it.
      We write $\Qsp$ for the invariant positive form on $W$ normalised to $k_x^2 + k_y^2 + k_z^2$ in an adapted
      orthonormal frame.

      The cotangent fibre of the product is $V = \Rtau\oplus W$, with coordinates $(k_\tau, k)$.

      \begin{itemize}
        \item[\textup{[ACT$_\tau$]}] \emph{Supplied action on the cotangent fibre.}
        $\SU(2)$ acts on $V$ trivially on $\Rtau$ and by the real spin-one representation on $W$.
      \end{itemize}

      Corollary~\ref{cor:no-new-rep} shows that [ACT$_\tau$] is the form taken by any action through the spatial
      factor once [ACT] is supplied.

  \section{The Ordering Direction as a Modelling Input}
    \label{sec:internalization}

    \subsection{The ordering increment and its step}

      Let $T$ denote the shift on real sequences indexed by depth, $(Tf)(n) := f(n+1)$; for fixed $b$,
      $T\sigma_b(n) = \sigma_b(n+1)$.

      \begin{modelling}[Ordering parameter and ordering derivative]
        \label{lem:internalization}
        The ordering coordinate is identified with BFS depth through
        \[
          \tau = n\,\Delta\tau,
        \]
        where $\Delta\tau > 0$ is the temporal step assigned to one cascade step.
        For a real sequence $f$ on the depth window that is the restriction of a $C^4$ function $F$ on $\Rtau$,
        $f(n) = F(n\,\Delta\tau)$, the ordering derivative is $\partial_\tau F$, and
        \[
          \frac{(T - \mathrm{Id})f}{\Delta\tau}(n) = \partial_\tau F(n\,\Delta\tau) + O(\Delta\tau).
        \]
        The step $\Delta\tau$ is a free parameter of the identification.
      \end{modelling}

      The displayed expansion is Taylor's formula; the content of Modelling identification~\ref{lem:internalization}
      is the choice of $\tau$ as a function of depth, which is the modelling choice of Q5b Remark~3.3, together with
      the explicit step that this choice requires.
      Without a step, $T - \mathrm{Id}$ is a difference operator on sequences and has no derivative as a limit.

      \begin{remark}
        The identification supplies a parametrisation of the ordering line and nothing more.
        First, the Carnot convergence of Q5b Theorem~3.2 rescales the spatial word metric by $n^{-1}$; it concerns the
        spatial factor and fixes neither the ordering factor nor $\Delta\tau$.
        Second, $T$ acts on real sequences indexed by depth; it defines no operator on the spin-one module or on any
        representation space of $\SU(2)$.
        Third, replacing $\Delta\tau$ by $s\,\Delta\tau$ with $s>0$ replaces the ordering derivative by
        $s^{-1}\partial_\tau$: the choice of $\Delta\tau$ is the choice of a temporal unit.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{The ordering line is a trivial isotypic component}

      \begin{corollary}[Trivial action on the ordering line]
        \label{cor:no-new-rep}
        Let $\SU(2)$ act on the product $\Rtau\times\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ through the spatial factor only,
        $g\cdot(\tau, x) = (\tau, g\cdot x)$.
        The induced action on the cotangent fibre is the direct sum of the trivial representation on the ordering
        line and the induced action on spatial covectors.
        If the latter is the real spin-one representation \textup{[ACT]}, then \textup{[ACT$_\tau$]} holds, and the
        ordering line introduces no irreducible representation other than the trivial one.
      \end{corollary}

      \begin{proof}
        The differential of $(\tau, x)\mapsto(\tau, g\cdot x)$ is block diagonal, with the identity on the $\tau$
        direction, so its transpose fixes $d\tau$ and preserves the annihilator of $\partial_\tau$.
        Modelling identification~\ref{lem:internalization} makes $\tau$ a parameter of the product, and an action
        through the spatial factor leaves it fixed.
      \end{proof}

  \section{Equivariance Does Not Fix the Temporal Step}
    \label{sec:equivariance}

    \begin{proposition}[Temporal rescalings commute with the spatial action]
      \label{prop:equivariance}
      Assume \textup{[ACT$_\tau$]}.
      For $s>0$ let $D_s$ act on $V$ by $k_\tau\mapsto s\,k_\tau$ and as the identity on $W$.
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[(a)] $D_s$ commutes with the action of every $g\in\SU(2)$.
        \item[(b)] If $Q$ is an invariant form, then $Q\circ D_s$ is invariant, and on the forms of
        Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(a)} it acts by $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(s^2\alpha,\beta)$.
        \item[(c)] For fixed discrete ordering data, a second-order ordering term has coefficient proportional to
        $\Delta\tau^2$: if the discrete operator is $f\mapsto -a\,(T - 2\,\mathrm{Id} + T^{-1})f$ with $a>0$, then
        on $f(n) = F(n\,\Delta\tau)$ it equals $-a\,\Delta\tau^2\,\partial_\tau^2 F + O(\Delta\tau^3)$, so
        $A_\tau = a\,\Delta\tau^2$.
        \item[(d)] Consequently no condition expressed as $\SU(2)$-equivariance of the ordering increment or as
        $\SU(2)$-invariance of the principal symbol fixes the ratio $-\alpha/\beta$.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      (a) $D_s$ is a scalar on the trivial summand and the identity on $W$, and the action preserves both summands.
      (b) $Q(D_s g v) = Q(g D_s v) = Q(D_s v)$ for invariant $Q$, and
      $(\alpha k_\tau^2 + \beta\Qsp)\circ D_s = s^2\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,\Qsp$.
      (c) Taylor expansion of $F((n\pm1)\Delta\tau)$ to fourth order gives
      $F(\tau+\Delta\tau) - 2F(\tau) + F(\tau-\Delta\tau) = \Delta\tau^2\,\partial_\tau^2F(\tau) + O(\Delta\tau^4)$.
      (d) The increments $(T-\mathrm{Id})/\Delta\tau$ and $(T-\mathrm{Id})/(s\,\Delta\tau)$ differ by the scalar
      factor $s$, which commutes with every linear action, so equivariance of the increment holds or fails
      independently of $\Delta\tau$.
      By (b), the set of invariant forms is stable under $D_s$, which changes $-\alpha/\beta$ by the factor $s^2$.
      An invariance or equivariance condition that held for one value of the ratio therefore holds for all.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}
      Spectral universality [U] (Q10 Definition~3.1~\cite{Beau2026q10}) is a statement about the spread of the
      fingerprint observable across blocks.
      It is open, and U1~\cite{Beau2026u1} proves an obstruction to one proposed route to it.
      It defines no $\SU(2)$ action on blocks or characters.
      By Proposition~\ref{prop:equivariance}, even an $\SU(2)$-equivariant ordering increment would leave
      $\Delta\tau$ free, so [U], whether or not it holds, does not bear on $A_\tau$ along this route.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Invariant Forms and Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}
    \label{sec:schur}

    \subsection{Invariant forms on the spin-one module}

      \begin{proposition}[Invariant forms on the real spin-one module]
        \label{prop:schur}
        Every $\SU(2)$-invariant symmetric bilinear form on $W$ is $\beta\,\Qsp$ with $\beta\in\mathbb{R}$, and it is
        positive definite exactly when $\beta>0$.
        The Casimir eigenvalue $2$ on the spin-one module refers to generators normalised by $[J_1,J_2] = iJ_3$ and
        cyclically; rescaling the generators by $\kappa$ multiplies it by $\kappa^2$.
        It therefore determines no value of $\beta$.
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        The complexification of $W$ is the irreducible complex spin-one module, so $W$ is of real type and every
        $\SU(2)$-equivariant endomorphism of $W$ is a real scalar~\cite{BrockerTomDieck1985}.
        An invariant symmetric bilinear form is $B(v,w) = \Qsp(v, Aw)$ with $A$ equivariant, hence
        $A = \beta\,\mathrm{Id}$.
        The Casimir statement is Q8 Lemma~4.1~\cite{BeauQ8}: $J_1^2+J_2^2+J_3^2$ is quadratic in the generators.
      \end{proof}

    \subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

      \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
(a) Let $B$ be an invariant symmetric bilinear form on $V = \Rtau\oplus W$ and let $e_\tau$ span $\Rtau$.
The map $\ell(w) := B(e_\tau, w)$ is a linear form on $W$, and invariance together with $g\,e_\tau = e_\tau$ gives
$\ell(g w) = \ell(w)$ for all $g$.
An invariant linear form on $W$ is an invariant vector of the dual module $W^*\cong W$, and the spin-one
module has no non-zero invariant vector, so $\ell = 0$: there is no mixed term.
The restriction of $B$ to $W$ is invariant, hence equal to $\beta\,\Qsp$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:schur}, and the
restriction to $\Rtau$ is $\alpha\,k_\tau^2$ for some real $\alpha$, with no condition since every scalar commutes with
the trivial action.
Conversely, $k_\tau^2$ and $\Qsp$ are invariant, so every form $\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,\Qsp$ is invariant.

(b) The form is diagonal with entries $\alpha$ on $\Rtau$ and $\beta$ on each of the three directions of an orthonormal
frame of $W$.
It has signature $(-,+,+,+)$, the negative direction being $\tau$, exactly when $\alpha<0<\beta$.
The pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ range over the whole open quadrant, so $-\alpha/\beta$ takes every positive value.

(c) In an adapted frame the co-metric of an extension is the matrix of its principal symbol.
By (a) an invariant symbol is $\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,\Qsp$, so the co-metric is
$\mathrm{diag}(\alpha,\beta,\beta,\beta)$, and $A_\tau = -\alpha$, $A_H = \beta$ in the sign convention
of~\eqref{eq:metric-intro}.
For fixed $\beta = 2\lambda$ every $\alpha<0$ gives an invariant Lorentzian symbol, so $A_\tau$ is unconstrained.
      \end{proof}

      Part (a) derives the absence of mixed temporal--spatial terms that Q8 Corollary~6.1 assumes, for an extension
      whose full four-dimensional symbol is invariant under [ACT$_\tau$].
      Part (c) reproduces the spatial conclusion $A_H = A_Z$ of Q8 Proposition~4.2 and adds that it carries no
      information about $A_\tau$.

    \subsection{Arbitrary actions on the four-dimensional fibre}

      The trivial action on the ordering line is a consequence of the product structure
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:no-new-rep}).
      One may ask whether a different action, mixing the ordering direction with space, could tie the two scalars
      together.
      It cannot while keeping a Lorentzian form.

      \begin{proposition}[No $\SU(2)$ action fixes a Lorentzian form up to scale]
        \label{prop:any-action}
        Let $\SU(2)$ act linearly and continuously on a real four-dimensional space $U$.
        Then $U$ is equivalent to exactly one of the following.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item[(i)] $U$ is the realification of the spin-$\tfrac12$ representation, and every invariant symmetric
          bilinear form is a real multiple of one positive definite form.
          \item[(ii)] $U$ is the sum of the trivial and the real spin-one representations, and the invariant symmetric
          bilinear forms form the two-dimensional family of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(a)}.
          \item[(iii)] $U$ is trivial, and every symmetric bilinear form is invariant.
        \end{enumerate}
        In particular, if an invariant non-degenerate indefinite form exists, the invariant forms do not reduce to a
        single ray, and no $\SU(2)$-invariance condition determines a Lorentzian co-metric up to an overall factor.
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        The group is compact, so $U$ carries an invariant inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and decomposes into
        irreducible real representations~\cite{BrockerTomDieck1985}.
        A real irreducible representation of a compact group is either of real type, with irreducible
        complexification, or the realification of a complex irreducible representation of complex or quaternionic
        type, of twice the complex dimension~\cite{BrockerTomDieck1985}.
        The complex irreducible representations of $\SU(2)$ are the spin-$j$ modules of dimension $2j+1$, of real
        type for integer $j$ and quaternionic type for half-integer $j$~\cite{FultonHarris1991}.
        The real irreducible representations of dimension at most four are therefore the trivial one (dimension one),
        the real spin-one representation (dimension three), and the realification $\mathbb{H}$ of spin $\tfrac12$
        (dimension four), in which unit quaternions act by left multiplication.
        The only decompositions of dimension four are $\mathbb{H}$, trivial plus spin one, and four trivial summands.

        Every invariant symmetric bilinear form is $B(v,w) = \langle v, Aw\rangle$ with $A$ equivariant and
        self-adjoint.
        In case (i) the equivariant endomorphisms of $\mathbb{H}$ are the right multiplications $R_p$,
        $p\in\mathbb{H}$, and the adjoint of $R_p$ is $R_{\bar p}$, so $R_p$ is self-adjoint exactly when $p$ is real
        and $B$ is a real multiple of the inner product.
        Case (ii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(a), whose family contains the Lorentzian forms with $\alpha<0<\beta$.
        In case (iii) there is no condition.
        A non-degenerate indefinite invariant form therefore exists only in cases (ii) and (iii), where the invariant
        forms span a space of dimension two or ten.
      \end{proof}

  \section{Consequences}
    \label{sec:consequences}

    \subsection{The co-metric with two independent scales}

      \begin{corollary}[Co-metric with two independent scales]
        \label{cor:metric}
        Assume the inputs of Q5b Theorem~5.2 \textup{([H-L], [H-lift])}, a supplied full-rank extension satisfying
        \textup{[T]} and \textup{[INV$^+$]} of Q8, and invariance of its four-dimensional principal symbol under
        \textup{[ACT$_\tau$]} with a negative ordering term.
        Then its co-metric in an adapted frame is
        \[
          g^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau,\, 2\lambda,\, 2\lambda,\, 2\lambda),
          \qquad A_\tau>0,\ \lambda>0,
        \]
        with $A_\tau$ and $\lambda$ independent, non-degenerate of rank four and of signature $(-,+,+,+)$.
        For every such pair, the ordering coordinate $\tau' := \tau\,(2\lambda/A_\tau)^{1/2}$ turns the co-metric into
        $2\lambda\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$ with $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$.
      \end{corollary}

      \begin{proof}
        Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(c) gives the diagonal form with independent entries, and Q8 Proposition~4.2 writes the
        common spatial value as $2\lambda$.
        With $\tau = s\,\tau'$ one has $k_{\tau'} = s\,k_\tau$, so $-A_\tau k_\tau^2 = -(A_\tau/s^2)\,k_{\tau'}^2$,
        and $s^2 = A_\tau/(2\lambda)$ gives $-2\lambda\,k_{\tau'}^2$.
      \end{proof}

      The last statement is the precise sense in which the ratio is a relative normalisation: every pair
      $(A_\tau,\lambda)$ becomes a multiple of $\eta$ after a choice of temporal unit, which by
      Modelling identification~\ref{lem:internalization} is a choice of $\Delta\tau$.
      The ratio acquires content only once $\Delta\tau$ is fixed independently in spatial units.
      The negative sign of the ordering term is an input: invariance does not fix it
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b)); Q5b stipulates it, and [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] makes it the only sign compatible
      with a finite characteristic cone ordered by $\tau$ (Q5b Theorem~6.1 and Remark~6.2).

      \begin{remark}[The number $2$ and the ratio]
The value $2$ is the Casimir eigenvalue of the spin-one module in one normalisation of the generators
(Proposition~\ref{prop:schur}); setting $A_H = 2$ or $A_\tau = 2$ is a choice of units, not a consequence of invariance.
What invariance leaves as the only non-conventional quantity is the ratio of the temporal and spatial scalars,
expressed in units fixed independently in each sector.
In a kinematic reading, in which $\tau$ measures time and the spatial coordinates measure length, the characteristic
cone of $-A_\tau\partial_\tau^2 + A_H\Delta$, with $\Delta$ the Laplacian of the spatial form $\Qsp$,
propagates at speed $(A_H/A_\tau)^{1/2}$, and the ratio is then the
square of an invariant speed.
That reading requires the metric and kinematic interpretation of both coordinates and is not used in the theorems.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{Status of Q5b-O3}

      \begin{remark}[Status of Q5b-O3]
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:metric}, and once the spatial scale $\lambda$ is fixed, the temporal half
of open problem Q5b-O3 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}, identifying $A_\tau$, amounts to supplying one datum that couples the
temporal and spatial normalisations: the ratio $A_\tau/A_H$, or, for fixed discrete ordering data $a$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:equivariance}(c)), the step $\Delta\tau$ expressed in spatial units.
Invariance under any linear $\SU(2)$ action, with or without [U], does not supply it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Propositions~\ref{prop:equivariance} and~\ref{prop:any-action}).
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{The typed chain}

      Each link toward a value of $A_\tau$ is listed with its status.
      \begin{center}
        \small
        \begin{tabular}{lll}
          Link & Content & Status \\
          \hline
          Foundation~\cite{Beau2026m} & time as projective ordering & interpretive \\
          Q5b Remark~3.3~\cite{Beau2026q5b} & product $\mathbb{R}_\tau\times\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ & modelling choice \\
          Modelling identification~\ref{lem:internalization} & $\tau = n\,\Delta\tau$ & modelling, $\Delta\tau$ free \\
          Q7, Q8 [ACT]~\cite{Beau2026q7,BeauQ8} & spin-one spatial action & supplied \\
          Corollary~\ref{cor:no-new-rep} & [ACT$_\tau$] from the product & proved \\
          Theorem~\ref{thm:main} & $\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,\Qsp$, no mixed term & proved \\
          Proposition~\ref{prop:any-action} & any action on four dimensions & proved \\
          Q8 Proposition~4.2~\cite{BeauQ8} & $A_H = A_Z = 2\lambda$ & proved under [T], [INV$^+$] \\

          Coupling datum & $\Delta\tau$ in spatial units & missing \\
        \end{tabular}
      \end{center}
      The chain stops at the coupling datum.
      No result shows that such a datum cannot exist; the verdict for the coefficient is that the element is missing,
      and the verdict for the representation-theoretic route is that it is ruled out.

    \subsection{Candidate sources of the coupling datum}

      The following candidates are exploratory proposals, not results of this paper or of the cited sources.
      Each would be an additional input, to be derived or tested separately.

      \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Saturation of the capacity bound.}
        The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026c} states the axiom [A-cap], $|\partial_\tau\phi|\le c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, which
        bounds an ordering rate.
        It is an inequality; it would fix the ratio only through an independently derived saturation rule relating
        $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ to the spatial normalisation.
        \item \emph{The discrete front.}
        The BFS shell at depth $n$ lies at word distance $n$, so the cascade front advances one unit of word length per
        step.
        Postulating that one step of $\tau$ equals one unit of spatial word length would fix $\Delta\tau$ in spatial
        units.
        This is a modelling postulate, and relating it to the quadratic symbol is itself non-trivial, since the limit
        metric of Q5b Theorem~3.2 is sub-Finsler rather than Riemannian and the Carnot rescaling acts on the spatial
        factor only.
        \item \emph{Ordering data.}
        The temporal-ordering paper~\cite{Beau2026pto} supports a monotone ordering proxy numerically; it supplies no
        step and no coefficient.
      \end{itemize}

  \section{Summary and Open Problems}
    \label{sec:summary}

    \subsection{What this paper establishes}

      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The ordering parameter is identified with BFS depth through $\tau = n\,\Delta\tau$ with a free step; this
        is a modelling identification, and the ordering derivative is defined relative to that step
        (Modelling identification~\ref{lem:internalization}).
        \item Under an action through the spatial factor, the ordering covector line carries the trivial
        representation (Corollary~\ref{cor:no-new-rep}, proved).
        \item Temporal rescalings commute with the spatial action and change the temporal coefficient, so no
        equivariance or invariance condition fixes the step (Proposition~\ref{prop:equivariance}, proved).
        \item Every invariant form on the fibre is $\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,\Qsp$ with independent scalars and no
        mixed term; a spatial normalisation yields no value of $A_\tau$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, proved).
        \item For every linear $\SU(2)$ action on four dimensions, the invariant forms are the multiples of one definite
        form or a family
        of dimension at least two (Proposition~\ref{prop:any-action}, proved).
        \item Under the inputs of Q5b and Q8, the co-metric is $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda)$
        with two independent scales (Corollary~\ref{cor:metric}, conditional), and the temporal half of Q5b-O3
        amounts to one coupling datum.
      \end{enumerate}

      The paper closes a derivation route, not the possibility $A_\tau = 2$.

    \subsection{Interpretive outlook}

      \emph{Status: interpretation, not a result.}
      In the reading in which $\tau$ is the parameter of projective ordering and the Heisenberg factor carries
      relational space, the results say that the symmetry of space and the rate of time are independent pieces of
      information.
      Spatial isotropy fixes the shape of the spatial form and forbids any preferred mixing of time with space, but the
      rate at which ordering advances relative to spatial relational distance, which a kinematic reading would call
      the invariant speed, is a further datum.
      This places the origin of the light-cone opening outside the representation theory of space: if the programme
      is to derive it, the derivation must come from how the ordering cascade and the spatial relations are coupled,
      for instance through a saturated capacity bound or through the advance of the breadth-first front.
      Emergent-Lorentz approaches reach the same conclusion from the dynamics of a medium; here it follows from Schur's
      lemma alone.

    \subsection{Open problems}

      \begin{description}
        \item[Q11-O1 (Coupling datum).]
        Supply, from the structure of the cascade, one datum coupling the temporal and spatial normalisations: the step
        $\Delta\tau$ in spatial units for fixed discrete ordering data, or directly the ratio $A_\tau/A_H$.
        \item[Q11-O2 (Sign of the ordering term).]
        Invariance fixes neither the ratio nor the sign of $\alpha$ relative to $\beta$
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b)); Q5b stipulates the Lorentzian sign, and [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] makes it the only
        sign compatible with a finite characteristic cone ordered by $\tau$ (Q5b Theorem~6.1 and Remark~6.2).
        Derive it from the ordering structure.
        \item[Q11-O3 (Front speed and symbol).]
        Relate the unit advance of the BFS front in word length to the principal symbol of a limit operator, taking into
        account that the Carnot limit of the spatial factor is sub-Finsler; decide whether this yields the coupling
        datum or only a bound.
      \end{description}
